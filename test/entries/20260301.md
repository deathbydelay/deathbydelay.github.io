# March 2026

## Radiation Begins

We visited a new dentist, specifically my ENTs personal dentist. The whole ride there I was on edge because I remembered all the torture I went through at the last dentist and it was just too much to deal with. Songs came on the radio that I would normally recite from start to finish, but I just couldn’t break out of that feeling of fear. Once we got to the dentist we waited our turn and they brought us in the back and took a little back story of what’s gone on with the teeth and the reason they had to be pulled, etc. They took some x-rays and we found out that there is a piece of a tooth still left on the upper right where the hole won’t close. I’m not sure if I was clear so I'll restate it. THERE IS A SMALL PIECE OF A ROOT OF A TOOTH LEFT WHERE THE HOLE WON’T CLOSE. Apologies, it’s just very frustrating. Anyway, the dentist comes in, we discuss what my ENT wants done and he calls him just to clarify. The dentist is examining the rest of my mouth and his nurse is mixing up the goop that’s going into the mold. It’s white, it’s viscous, and it looks like it’s going to taste like chalk. It has to be done, but mentally I'm thinking it’s going to be gross, I don't want to do it. As he scrapes the goop into the plate that will be used to make an impression I just closed my eyes and let it happen. I open my mouth and as he’s moving it towards me I catch a minty scent. He helps guide the plate in and as the goop touches my gums it feels cool, it tastes minty, it’s pleasant and not like chalk at all, lol. It takes a bit for the cast to set, but it’s out shortly after it’s in. We're told we can pick up the bridge plate in the coming days when we come to radiation.

I noticed a bit of swelling on my right cheek near the ear. There is a bit of pain, but I suspect it's sinus related since I woke up clogged a couple days in a row. Pollen count had been high so that was probably the reason since it didn’t persist.

I started radiation and immediately I could taste something strange and horrible. It was constant at first and I thought I had just eaten something foul or had a sinus infection or something. I later learned that it’s a metallic taste and smell that a lot of people who go through radiation of the face and neck get. I’ve had a few days where I felt nauseous, but most days I feel a bit tired in the evening. I’ve lost a bit of time throughout the day as I've got to do these baking soda rinses 3 times a day. I take a teaspoon of baking soda and add it to about 12oz of water and swish or gargle the whole cup. I've been wearing this dental plate/obturator nearly all day. I couldn’t remember if the dentist told me that I needed to keep it in while eating so I had been taking it out. I tried one day to eat with it in and while I could it made swallowing difficult because of how thick the plate is. It narrows the space on the roof of my mouth which makes it hard to swallow. It makes it feel as if I need to chew my food smaller so I would move it back to the two molars. I have to break it down more then try to swallow again and repeat ad nauseum. Since it’s so difficult I went back to taking it out to eat and do radiation. To combat food getting into the hole that the obturator is covering I just do one of my baking soda rinses after I eat since I really only ever eat two meals a day. Since all this started I think I'm down about 25-30 pounds overall.

I also learned that the 15 minutes per session is really an incorrect assessment of the time spent at the office. Some days I'll wait up to 15 minutes after my appointment start time just to get into the back where I'll receive my radiation treatment. I want to say that most of the time I'm only doing radiation treatments, but I've also seen the doctor once a week and I've seen a nutritionist and a social worker. One time a machine was down as well, so there are all sorts of things that could happen that could extend the appointment. Regardless, I've been tracking durations for traveling to the appointment, the appointment itself, and traveling from the appointment to my house.

As far as radiation goes, the appointment is simple. They come to the waiting room to get me, then as we’re walking back to the machine I'm tucking the collar of my polo in because it gets in the way of the mask and can throw off my alignment to the machine. Then they ask my DoB, and I take my dental plate out and put the bite block with the tongue depressor in. Then I lay on the table and scoot up or down to align myself with the head rest. Then they put the mask over my head and I have to adjust further to get in the right spot as they are clamping me to the table. Once I'm clamped and in the correct position they start the treatment procedure. The machine is called a linear accelerator and the first part of the treatment is a mini-CT scan. These small panels rotate towards me and the machine rotates around the table I'm laying on. Once that mini CT is done the panels rotate away and I can hear a thunk in the room towards the entrance of the room, and when the machine starts to rotate around there is a high pitch noise along with some muted mechanical noises coming from the radiation head. My vision on the right side is mostly occluded so it took me a few sessions to pick up on what’s going on and what is making those mechanical noises. I figured out that in the part that sticks out over me there is an iris or aperture where the radiation is emitted. That aperture has a bunch of these thin pieces of plastic that interlock with each other and move in and out to change the shape of the radiation beam. When you go for your initial scans they create a 3d model of the area they are trying to radiate and that is what the machine is changing shape for, but it’s changing the shape of the aperture as it’s rotating so it only radiates the part that needs it. What took me so long to figure this out is because even though I can kind of see things on my left side, the aperture is covered in glass and that makes it difficult to see the fingers moving since I'm reflected in the glass too. Eventually I was able to confirm that’s what was going on when I asked one of the radiation techs.

It’s kind of a nice 10 minutes or so where nobody is talking to me or asking me questions or stressing me out. At first I had trouble with saliva building up and triggering a swallow reflex, but I've since learned that it’s nothing to worry about since the treatment is over soon.

I think the biggest shock was losing hair in the back of my head. I always suspected that the machine radiating back there was going to cause something, but I figured it would be more sunburn like on my face. I’m not really concerned because hair grows back, but I can't fix it right now since getting a haircut or shaving it will mess with how the mask I'm under fits.

I did meet with a nutritionist that the Radiation Lab has available for everyone under their care and she mentioned that my suspicions about texture, aromatics and temperatures were correct since that’s basically all that’s left once you’ve lost your taste.
